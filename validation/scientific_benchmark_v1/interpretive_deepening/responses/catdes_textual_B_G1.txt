Statistical anchor:
Relative to the full sample, G1 selects price more frequently as the primary concern (43.75% versus 23.96%) and the conventional-diet modality more frequently (62.50% versus 44.79%). Environment (9.38% versus 27.08%) and quality (15.62% versus 29.17%) are less frequently selected as primary concerns. The group has lower means for monthly spending (103.12 versus 130.52), purchase frequency (2.85 versus 3.50), age (34.41 versus 40.26), and household size (2.25 versus 2.75).

Textual enrichment:
The comment that the price is manageable despite limited choice (TXT000003) gives a qualified practical meaning to the statistically greater selection of price as a primary concern. It suggests that attention to price can coexist with appreciation of familiar taste, rather than requiring respondents to reject the offer. The remarks do not show that price concern causes the lower average spending or purchasing frequency, and they do not explain conventional diet, age or household size.

Additional insights:
The comments introduce considerations absent as positive statistical markers: convenience after work (TXT000004), ingredient-quality questions (TXT000004), limited choice (TXT000003), and a wish for clearer environmental-impact information (TXT000001). Importantly, an environmental-information request does not contradict the relative underrepresentation of environment as the selected *main* concern. One concerns what some people express; the other is a statistical comparison of primary-response frequencies.

Internal diversity:
The small set of formulations includes favorable judgments about affordability, taste and convenience alongside different reservations about choice, information and ingredients. One environmental-impact assessment is explicitly qualified as not applying to every purchase (TXT000011). Thirty-two comments come from only four distinct formulations, so neither the apparent convergence nor any one concern can safely be generalized as unanimous.

Contextualized profile:
The most informative combined reading is **qualified everyday acceptance under price attention**: G1 is statistically marked by greater relative price salience and lower purchasing activity, while the comments depict familiar, manageable and convenient food choices that remain open to scrutiny about choice, ingredients and environmental information. This interpretation contextualizes the price-related marker and adds separate concerns; it does not turn those concerns into group-level statistical traits or explain the other CATDES differences.

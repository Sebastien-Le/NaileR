## Integrated sensory reading of the evaluated chocolates

The profiles reveal more than a single cocoa-versus-milk opposition. Flavor/aroma and texture jointly organize the products, but not always in the same way: some products are cocoa-oriented and crunchy, another is sweet and crunchy without a retained cocoa signature, and the markedly milky/sweet product is also more melting and less crunchy. These are recurring descriptive combinations of retained attributes relative to the average product, **not** statistically established correlations among attributes or pairwise tests between products.

**choc1 — a concentrated cocoa/bitter and drying configuration.** Higher CocoaF (adjusted mean 8.07), CocoaA (7.09), bitterness (7.07), astringency (4.76), and acidity (4.66) occur with lower MilkF (1.57), MilkA (3.59), sweetness (3.14), caramel (1.67), and vanilla (1.10). The important configuration is the alignment of cocoa-related notes with bitter, astringent and acidic sensations and the reduced prominence of sweet/milky markers. This is a multidimensional sensory description, not a hedonic judgment or an explanation of formulation.

**choc2 — crunchiness accompanied by cocoa notes rather than melting.** Higher crunchiness (7.71) and CocoaF (6.91), together with lower melting (4.33), MilkF (2.38), sweetness (4.62), and caramel (2.78), suggest a relatively dry-textured, crunchy cocoa-oriented configuration. Here 'dry-textured' refers only to the reported low melting/high crunch combination, not to an independently measured dryness attribute. No retained evidence identifies choc2 as particularly bitter or astringent.

**choc3 — sweet/milky sensations coupled with a melting texture.** Higher MilkF (7.71), MilkA (6.05), sweetness (7.60), caramel (6.33), vanilla (3.67), and melting (7.31) converge with lower CocoaF (3.38), CocoaA (4.67), bitterness (1.40), astringency (1.21), acidity (1.57), crunchiness (2.98), and granularity (1.60); stickiness (5.03) is higher. The joint profile is distinctive because its aromatic/taste character and reported texture reinforce the same sensory impression: comparatively milky, sweet and melting, rather than strongly cocoa-bitter or crunchy.

**choc4 — sharper sensory notes with granularity and less melting.** Higher acidity (3.93), astringency (3.69), bitterness (5.19), and granularity (3.55), alongside lower MilkF (2.59), sweetness (4.29), caramel (2.67), and melting (4.38), create a different configuration from choc1. Both display some bitter/astringent aspects, but choc4's retained signature combines these especially with granularity and acidity; this does not establish an inferential product-by-product contrast.

**choc5 — cocoa aroma/flavor combined with crunch and less stickiness.** Higher CocoaA (6.79), CocoaF (6.79), and crunchiness (6.64), alongside lower stickiness (3.22), constitute a compact cross-modal profile. The limited selected evidence does not support an interpretation of its sweetness, milk notes, bitterness, or melting relative to the average.

**choc6 — a relatively sweet but crunchy, less melting configuration.** Higher crunchiness (7.33) and sweetness (5.62), together with lower melting (4.21) and acidity (2.67), show why crunchy products cannot all be characterized as cocoa-led or less sweet: the selected markers here describe a different taste–texture combination from choc2 or choc5. The absence of a retained CocoaF or MilkF marker does not mean those sensations are absent.

## What emerges from the joint configuration

Two complementary readings are justified. First, choc1 and especially choc3 illustrate a broad opposition between cocoa/bitter/astringent and milk/sweet/caramel/melting notes, but this opposition does not fully characterize every product. Second, **crunchiness recurs across choc2, choc5, and choc6 with different accompanying flavor markers**, while the more melting configuration is especially pronounced in choc3. Thus texture helps refine the flavor-led interpretation rather than merely repeating it. Choc4 adds a distinct bitter/astringent–granular combination, and choc5/choc6 require narrower claims because fewer markers were retained.

This integrated interpretation is a conceptual organization of the product-specific statistical descriptors; it does not establish sensory mechanisms, a single latent axis, causality, formulation differences, consumer liking, preference, quality, or direct pairwise statistical significance.

## Réponse XQ72

## G1 — Lower purchasing activity alongside greater relative price salience

G1 combines more frequent selection of price as the main concern (43.75% versus 23.96% for the full sample) with lower average monthly spending (103.12 versus 130.52) and purchase frequency (2.85 versus 3.50). Considered jointly, these results suggest an economically salient, less purchase-active group-level configuration, rather than just a collection of separate deviations. The conjunction is descriptive: the results do not show that those choosing price as their main concern are the same individuals who spend or purchase less, or that concern about price explains these averages.

The conventional-diet modality is more frequent (62.50% versus 44.79%), while environmental (9.38% versus 27.08%) and quality (15.62% versus 29.17%) responses are less frequently selected as the main concern. This makes the relative hierarchy of reported concerns and dietary practices part of G1's statistical profile, not evidence that environmental or quality considerations are absent. Lower mean age (34.41 versus 40.26) and household size (2.25 versus 2.75) add demographic distinctions, but no mechanism connecting those variables to purchasing patterns is established.

## G2 — Greater purchasing involvement with higher reported satisfaction

G2 has higher monthly spending (159.22 versus 130.52), purchase frequency (4.32 versus 3.50), and satisfaction (7.24 versus 6.46). The most informative joint reading is that greater recorded purchasing activity and a more favorable reported experience coexist at the group level: this profile is not simply defined by how often or how much its members buy. The statistical summaries do not establish an individual-level association between these characteristics or show that buying more creates satisfaction.

Online purchasing is more frequent than in the full sample (46.88% versus 31.25%), as are flexitarian responses (59.38% versus 40.62%), while conventional-diet responses are less frequent (21.88% versus 44.79%). These features broaden the profile to channels and dietary practice, but should not be folded into an unverified single motivation or lifestyle. G2 also has higher mean age (47.12 versus 40.26) and household size (3.38 versus 2.75), which remain demographic characteristics rather than explanations for the observed differences.

## G3 — A narrower, satisfaction-related distinction

The only selected characteristic for G3 is lower mean satisfaction (5.88 versus 6.46). That finding is substantive but provides no basis for attributing to G3 the purchasing, dietary, concern, or demographic configuration of either of the other groups. It would be misleading to construct a full group identity from one retained marker.

## Integrative interpretation

Across the displayed profiles, the most interpretable contrast is between G1's relatively greater price salience and lower purchase activity and G2's higher purchase activity coupled with higher satisfaction and a distinct distribution of channels and diets. These are two multidimensional group-level configurations, not tested pairwise contrasts or inferred individual pathways. G3 does not complete a three-step continuum: its sparse retained evidence establishes only a difference in satisfaction relative to the whole sample. The substantive value of the joint reading is to show which characteristics form a reasonably coherent descriptive configuration **and which do not yet warrant one**.

## Réponse MR18

**G1 — An economically attentive profile with relatively lower purchasing activity.** Price is more frequently reported as the main concern in G1 than in the full sample (43.75% versus 23.96%), although it is not a majority response. Environmental concern (9.38% versus 27.08%) and quality concern (15.62% versus 29.17%) are less represented; this does not imply that group members disregard either issue. A conventional diet is more frequent (62.50% versus 44.79%). These categorical findings accompany lower mean monthly spending (103.12 versus 130.52), purchase frequency (2.85 versus 3.50), age (34.41 versus 40.26), and household size (2.25 versus 2.75). Together, they support a descriptive profile in which economic attention coincides with comparatively limited observed purchasing expenditure and frequency. They do not establish that price concern causes these differences.

**G2 — A more purchase-active, comparatively satisfied and flexitarian-oriented profile.** The clearest configuration combines a higher frequency of the flexitarian modality (59.38% versus 40.62%) with a lower frequency of the conventional modality (21.88% versus 44.79%), and a higher frequency of online purchasing (46.88% versus 31.25%). Members of G2 also have higher group means for monthly spending (159.22 versus 130.52), purchase frequency (4.32 versus 3.50), satisfaction (7.24 versus 6.46), age (47.12 versus 40.26), and household size (3.38 versus 2.75). These results suggest a comparatively engaged purchasing profile involving several channels and dietary practices, not a single demonstrated motivation. In particular, online purchasing is more common than in the full sample but remains below half of G2.

**G3 — Lower reported satisfaction, with otherwise limited characterization.** The only retained finding shown is a lower mean satisfaction score (5.88 versus 6.46 for the full sample). This is meaningful but insufficient to infer G3's purchasing habits, dietary priorities, demographics, or a coherent behavioral identity. Absence of other selected markers in this prompt is not proof that G3 resembles the overall sample on those characteristics.

**Overall synthesis.** G1 is more clearly marked by price concern and lower purchasing measures, while G2 combines higher purchasing measures, higher satisfaction, more frequent flexitarian responses, and relatively greater online use. G3 is distinguishable here only by lower satisfaction. These are descriptions relative to the overall sample, not statistically tested pairwise comparisons among G1, G2 and G3. The uneven quantity of retained evidence should temper the apparent sharpness of the group portraits, especially for G3.

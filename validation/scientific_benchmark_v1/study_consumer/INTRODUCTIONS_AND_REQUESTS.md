# Shared study introduction

This study examines food-purchasing behavior in 96 consumers from the same population, organized into three neutral groups named G1, G2 and G3. The data combine quantitative measures, categorical characteristics, and individual open comments. The purpose is to characterize patterns while preserving within-group heterogeneity and distinguishing observations from interpretive hypotheses.

# CONDES request

Characterize the substantive profile associated with satisfaction from the statistical associations shown. Synthesize convergent facts, keep interpretation traceable, and distinguish observed associations from broader hypotheses. Do not infer causality.

# CATDES request

For each group, describe the main coherent profile from the displayed statistical facts. Distinguish central interpretive patterns from secondary characteristics and do not infer causality or individual properties from group-level results.

# DESCFREQ request

Describe the main frequency profile of the groups from the displayed relative frequencies. Synthesize convergent patterns without treating a frequency difference as an absolute preference or causal effect.

# TEXTUAL request

Identify recurring themes and meaningful tensions in the comments. Synthesize several responses when justified, preserve minority positions and distinguish what respondents literally expressed from contextual interpretation. Do not infer hidden motivations.

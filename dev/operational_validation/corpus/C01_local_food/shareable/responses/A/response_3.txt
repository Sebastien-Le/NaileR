Group 3: The Pragmatic Individualists

Group 3 is characterized by a strong emphasis on personal choice, pleasure, and diversity in food, coupled with a general skepticism towards extensive mandates, particularly those related to environmental regulations and broad socio-economic redistribution. This group tends to exhibit a more pragmatic or neutral stance on various governance approaches, yet displays clear opposition to certain interventions. An overrepresentation of "Employees" suggests a working demographic.

**Central Interpretive Pattern: Prioritizing Personal Freedom and Pleasure, Resisting Mandates**

A defining characteristic is the group's staunch defense of individual food choices and pleasure. They are significantly more likely to declare "Eating is a pleasure and I don’t want to compromise on that" as "Totally agree" (22.64% vs 14.49%) and to "Totally agree" with "I’m not ready to give up on the diversity of the food I eat" (13.84% vs 9.77%). This translates into a strong rejection of restrictive food policies, such as "Limit meat to two days a week in collective catering" (23.27% "Totally unacceptable" vs 13.26%). While they support imposing "a baseline amount of traditional local recipes" and are more open to "Regularly impose dishes from other cultures in collective catering," these appear to be about enriching their experience rather than restricting it. They are also more accepting of banning ultra-processed products, which aligns with health considerations without necessarily compromising pleasure or diversity.

This preference for personal liberty extends to a notable resistance to various mandates and regulations:
*   **Environmental Regulations:** They are distinctly less inclined to prioritize biodiversity over yield ("Unacceptable" MORE FREQUENT, "Totally acceptable" LESS FREQUENT). They show significant opposition to imposing optimized energy management ("Unacceptable" MORE FREQUENT), removing synthetic chemicals from agriculture ("Totally unacceptable" MORE FREQUENT), and exclusive reliance on renewable energy ("Unacceptable" MORE FREQUENT). They also show more opposition to mandatory recycling.
*   **Socio-Economic Interventions:** The group expresses higher levels of "Unacceptable" or "Totally unacceptable" for policies like "Redistribute the world’s food and water resources fairly," "Impose a fair redistribution of profits throughout the food chain," and "Support, through taxes, aid programs for disadvantaged regions and populations." They also strongly reject taxing food not produced in France and an economic model based on barter.

**Nuanced Stance on Governance and Innovation**

While resistant to many mandates, Group 3 is not dogmatically anti-intervention across the board. They demonstrate a relative openness to trusting science and innovation: they are more likely to find "Trust science in the development of innovative technologies" as "Acceptable" or "Totally acceptable." They are also more open to requiring the food industry to increase product references for specific diets.

Their view on decision-making bodies is pragmatic: they show less strong opposition ("Neither acceptable nor unacceptable" is MORE FREQUENT, "Totally unacceptable" LESS FREQUENT) to local, community, and even State authorities deciding on food policies, and also to being monitored solely by NGOs. This suggests less ideological alignment with specific governance models and perhaps a more 'wait and see' or less opinionated attitude compared to the full sample, although they are notably more opposed to citizen councils having veto power.

**Complex Views on Education and Societal Roles**

A key nuance arises in their views on education. Group 3 strongly supports "Impose schools to teach the importance of good nutrition" ("Totally acceptable" MORE FREQUENT). However, they are simultaneously more likely to "Agree" with the statement "I’m afraid children are indoctrinated by school." This highlights a desire for practical, beneficial education, while maintaining vigilance against ideological influence. Their stance on "Impose civic education courses" is also notably polarized, with both "Totally acceptable" and "Totally unacceptable" being MORE FREQUENT, indicating internal divisions or a highly conditional acceptance.

They show mixed views on civic duties; less opposition to mandatory urban garden maintenance and civic service, but also more outright rejection of civic service. Furthermore, a higher proportion of this group "Agree" with "I don’t have the time to improve my habits," suggesting practical constraints that might contribute to their resistance to behavioral mandates.

**Conclusion**

Group 3 presents as **"Pragmatic Individualists."** Their profile is reasonably coherent around the core theme of individual freedom, particularly regarding food pleasure and diversity, and a consistent resistance to mandates that restrict personal choice or enforce broad societal restructuring (environmental, economic redistribution). They trust scientific progress and adopt a more neutral, less dogmatic stance on various governance structures. The group's profile is somewhat ambiguous due to some internal contradictions, particularly in their views on education and civic engagement, where strong support for practical learning coexists with skepticism about potential indoctrination and polarized opinions on civic duties. Despite these nuances, the overarching pattern points to a group that values personal autonomy and pleasure, while being selective and often skeptical about the scope and imposition of external regulations and policies.

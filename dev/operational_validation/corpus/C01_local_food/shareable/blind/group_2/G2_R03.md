# Blind interpretation G2_R03

Group 2 comprises individuals who are older and more established than the average participant. This is evidenced by a lower frequency of those aged 18 to 29 and students, and a higher frequency of individuals aged 50 to 64, married, and belonging to the "Executives and higher intellectual professions" or "Retired" socio-professional categories.

The beliefs of this group are marked by a profound skepticism towards centralized authority and imposition, coupled with a strong inclination towards localism and traditional values in the food system. They show a clear rejection of the State or local authorities making unilateral decisions on food policies, tending strongly towards finding such propositions "Totally unacceptable." This distrust extends to scientific authority, with a higher frequency of finding "Trust science in the development of innovative technologies" unacceptable and a greater agreement with "I don’t trust science when it comes to my diet." They also exhibit skepticism towards rigid, long-term policies and the mandatory imposition of civic services or annual health checkups.

In terms of food systems, these individuals strongly favor local production and traditional practices. They are significantly more likely to support taxing food not produced and processed in France and to systematically favor supply from small producers and family farms. A preference for local and familiar is also apparent in their strong rejection of regularly imposing dishes from other cultures in collective catering. While they are environmentally conscious, prioritizing respect for biodiversity over yield and supporting the removal of synthetic chemicals from agriculture, their approach is pragmatic. For instance, they show more hesitation or even unacceptability towards the idea of *only* using renewable energy, and a stronger belief that major polluters, rather than individuals, should make the most environmental efforts.

Regarding social and economic fairness, they strongly support a fair redistribution of profits throughout the food chain. However, their enthusiasm for broader redistributive measures, such as tax-funded aid programs for disadvantaged regions or populations, or global food and water resource redistribution, is significantly less pronounced, often leaning towards a neutral stance rather than outright acceptance. They also show greater acceptance for banning ultra-processed products and favoring bulk sales, indicating a preference for healthier, less industrialized food choices.

Overall, Group 2 demonstrates a profile that values autonomy, local control, and a more traditional, natural approach to food, while remaining wary of top-down regulations, scientific innovations, and extensive social or global interventions, particularly when they involve imposition.

Based on these characteristics, Group 2 can be named: **2. Autonomous Traditionalists**.

This name reflects the group's preference for self-determination and local control ("Autonomous") over centralized authority and global mandates. "Traditionalists" captures their skepticism towards innovative technologies, their rejection of imposing foreign cultural dishes, and their strong support for local, established food production methods like small producers and taxing imports, which often align with established cultural practices and values. The name also subtly acknowledges their pragmatic yet environmentally aware stance, which often seeks to preserve existing natural orders rather than embrace radical new solutions.
